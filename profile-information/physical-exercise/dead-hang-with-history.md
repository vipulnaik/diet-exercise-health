# Dead hang (with history)

Reference article: https://www.dmoose.com/blogs/training/what-is-a-dead-hang

## Goal and functional relevance of exercise

### Build towards being able to go both underhand and overhand for at least 1 minute each, when both are done in succession

A dead hang is a good way to improve the strength of my arms and
shoulders, while continuing to reinforce hand grip. It also helps with
balance and flexibility.

This will help me be able to carry and move heavier loads while also
stay balanced in more complex movements.

I can already do overhand for well over a minute, but the underhand
that I do following it is not consistently over a minute (as of
2026-09-29, it ranges between 44 and 88 seconds). So, I still have
some ways to go to build.

There is significant overlap in goals with other strength exercises,
such as [pull-ups](pull-ups-with-history.md),
[push-ups](push-ups-with-history.md), and
[weight-lifting](weight-lifting-exercises-with-history.md).

## Current practice

Each oscillation is about 2.2 to 2.5 seconds (based on both
measurement I did and estimation based on the 2pi sqrt{L/g} using an
estimate of L as the height from the fist I am hanging off of to my
center of mass).

Generally, I do this after returning from jogging.

* Overhand dead hang (palms away from me) for as long as I can; as of
  2026-09-29, this is 40 to 55 oscillations which is about 88 to 138
  seconds.

* Underhand dead hang (palms facing me) for as long as I can; as of
  2026-09-29, this is 20 to 35 oscillations which is about 44 to 88
  seconds.

## History

### History of oscillation ranges

In the table below, the lower and upper end of the typical range of
number of oscillations is provided. I do not separately measure time,
only the number of oscillations, so time estimates can be obtained
using the multiplier of 2.2 to 2.5 seconds.

Unlike some balance exercises, there is no retry if my number of
oscillations is below the threshold, so the documentation of these
ranges doesn't commit me to expensive retries. However, a higher range
still has a slight anchoring effect, pushing me to continue a little
longer than I otherwise would. Given how unpleasant the tail end of a
dead hang is, though, I try to be conservative about these ranges and
only increment them when I have a strong history of consistently
clearing the lower end of the range.

Further notes:

* On 2025-01-26, I noted that the number of oscillations for overhand
  and underhand in a session correlate. In other words, the sessions
  where I have more oscillations for the overhand dead hang tend to
  also have more oscillations for the underhand dead hang. The gap
  between the two numbers on a given day is usually around 20.

  However, this correlation pattern does not seem to have held up over
  time, and as of 2026-09-29 I consider the number of oscillations for
  overhand and underhand dead hang to be close to uncorrelated (as
  opposed to being correlated positively or negatively).

* The 2026-09-29 update is interesting in that the overhand dead hang
  range didn't increase; in fact, the lower end stayed the same and
  the upper end shrank from 60 t0 55. This is based on a review of
  recent overhead dead hang performance that has almost always stayed
  in the range of 40 to 55 oscillations. The upper end of 60 that I
  had set on 2026-04-11 had been based on recent upward outlier
  performance that has not been repeated.

### History of settings

#### 2024-10-02 onward: started underhand dead hang

On 2024-10-02, I started the underhand dead hang. To do this, I check
the time on my laptop, go to the dead hang bar, hang from it till I
can, then get off and check the time. The process of going from my
laptop to the bar and back takes 5-10 seconds, and the overall clocked
time is about 40-45 seconds, so my estimate of time with the dead hang
is about 30-35 seconds.

#### 2024-10-06 onward: introducing overhand dead hang as well

Starting 2024-10-06, I introduced the overhand dead and got an
estimate of about 45-50 seconds.

#### 2025-01-26 status update

As of 2025-01-26, I record dead hang based on the number of
oscillations rather than the duration because it's easier to measure
the number of oscillations. My empirical calculation of one
oscillation is that it's somewhere between 2.2 and 2.5 seconds.

Some time around this time, I solidified the practice of always
starting with overhand dead hang and then proceeding to underhand dead
hang, with a break of a few minutes.

### History of triggers for overall exercise

#### Initial alternation with weight-lifting

When I started the exercise on 2024-10-02, I would do it after
jogging, and on alternating days with days I did weight-lifting and
push-ups.

#### 2024-12-06: every 3 days setting

Starting 2024-12-06, I switched to the practice of doing this every 3
days I did exercise, and more specifically on the days after my
strength exercise break and the day before I do weight-lifting
exercises.

I always did this exercise after jogging. I would follow the exercise
with [pull-ups](pull-ups-with-history.md), that I started on
2024-11-14. The practice of doing this after pull-ups was formally
codified on 2025-01-26.
