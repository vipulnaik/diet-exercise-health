## Symptoms

### Sore throat / dryness (peaked over the period from 2026-11-23 to 2026-11-25)

This was the most central symptom. The evolution of the symptom is
discussed in the chronology of events.

### Sneezing and runny nose (2026-11-24 onward, peaking around 2026-11-26 and 2026-11-27)

I had a slightly increased rate of sneezing and runny nose. The
increased sneezing likely started 2026-11-24 (many of the sneezes were
outdoors so they didn't make it to Slack self-chat logs) and peaked
around the evening/night of 2026-11-26. Gradual mucus build-up peaked
around 2026-11-26 evening and 2026-11-27 morning and early afternoon,
with regular nose blowing and a reduced frequency of sneezing on
2026-11-27.

### Intermittent dry (throat-driven) coughing

I never had excessive or continuous coughing, but I did have
intermittent dry coughing that felt throat-driven rather than
chest-driven.

### Head/neck/shoulder soreness and reduced strength (seen on 2026-11-25 and 2026-11-26)

I experienced increased soreness of the head, neck, and shoulder. This
was not enough to detract or debilitate me in day-to-day activities
(such as carrying groceries or doing meal prep) but showed up in
reduced performance on hand gripper exercises.

### Increased system-level fatigue and slowness (seen 2026-11-25 onward, may have been present earlier)

My jogging attempt on 2026-11-25, the first after the onset of
symptoms, was shorter than usual (10 minutes compared to the usual of
over 30 minutes for indoor jogging). My jogging duration on 2026-11-26
was about 20 minutes, which is in between the 2026-11-25 level and my
norm.

It is possible that the fatigue increase started on 2026-11-24, but I
skipped exercises on that day for scheduling reasons, so I don't have
a similar readout.

I also felt a little bit slower and lazier when doing cognitive work,
though the effect was more around activation and context switching
than doing tasks when in flow.

### Increased light and sound sensitivity

My light and sound sensitivity were a little higher than usual. For
instance, I decided to put hands over ears in response to sirens from
ambulances passing by, both outdoors and indoors, on Tuesday
2026-11-24 and Wednesday 2026-11-25. This isn't a bad idea in general,
but it's not something I have felt the need to do in the recent
past. I also felt a little more aversion to having the light on late
at night during cooking and meal prep.

### No changes to food taste or smell

With my [June 2025 respiratory
infection](../2025/2025-06-respiratory-infection-likely-common-cold.md),
I had, prior to getting the runny nose, experienced weird changes to
the taste and smell of food. I did not experience any changes to the
taste and smell of food this time around. This was something I was
deliberately on the lookout for.

### Tuesday 2026-11-24: protective measures

On Tuesday 2026-11-24, now that the sore throat sensation was

## Diagnosis: likely common cold

### Key reasons to suspect a common cold over the flu or COVID

Based on a review of the evidence by myself and in a [private Claude
conversation](https://claude.ai/chat/cd774604-cb99-4ef3-aaac-9d7c2478518f),
I determined that this was likely a common cold.

* Base rates are much more supportive of a common cold than flu or
  COVID. I am vaccinated for both flu and COVID, though not in this
  season, so being vaccinated may not mean much.

* The symptom pattern matches the common cold more than the flu
  or COVID; see
  [here](https://pharmacy.msu.edu/resources/how-to-tell-cold-and-flu-symptoms-apart). For
  instance:

  * My symptom onset was gradual, as opposed to sudden for the flu.

  * The particular pattern of the sore throat improving and then mucus
    secretion increasing about 24 hours later is very characteristic
    of a common cold.

  * I didn't have a fever; fever is rare for the common cold but
    normal for the flu and COVID.

  * I had runny nose, sneezing, sore throat, and (occasional) cough --
    all symptoms that are common in the common cold and sometimes /
    rare for flu and COVID.

  * My symptoms fit the "above-the-neck" archetype. For instance, my
    coughing felt dry and throat-driven rather than chest-driven.

### Question mark about source of exposure

I was not in contact with any other humans over the period of
2026-11-20 to 2026-11-22, as I did not leave my home except doing
laundry in the in-building laundry room on 2026-11-22 (that was not
occupied or in active use by others when I did laundry). I did
exercises indoors (it was raining on 2026-11-20 and 2026-11-21). My
symptoms started 2026-11-23 before my first outdoor trip on 2026-11-23
to the Amazon Hub Locker. This raises the question of how I got
exposed.

There are a few possibilities:

* Contact with infected surfaces in the laundry room on 2026-11-22,
  which feels unlikely but is plausible. This would mean a fairly
  rapid symptom onset.

* The Trader Joe's trip on 2026-11-19. I was masked for the duration
  in store (except briefly at entry and exit) and sanitized my hands
  and didn't touch my face around the time of the trip. The long gap
  from the trip to symptom onset also argues against this being the
  source. But it's not completely ruled out. I do not remember being
  in proximity to anybody coughing or sneezing or otherwise visibly
  ill.

* The Amazon Hub Locker trip, after my initial fairly mild symptoms,
  delivered the actual viral infection, and the previous symptoms were
  not directly symptoms of infection. This feels unlikely, as I was
  masked in the Amazon Hub Locker location as well, not close to
  anybody else for most of the time, and not in the store for too long
  anyway. But it can't be ruled out. I do not remember being in
  proximity to anybody coughing or sneezing or otherwise visibly ill.

* Some other surface-driven infection (the surface of food purchased
  2026-11-19 or earlier may have had viruses that I came in contact
  with over subsequent days).

Each of these individually, and even all of them together, feel very
unlikely. The source of the infection therefore feels like a
mystery. However, these still make a common cold *relatively* more
likely than the flu or COVID, insofar as a common cold can transmit
more readily via surfaces compared to COVID.

### Comparison with non-respiratory-infection explanations

Some kind of irritation or allergy that is not infectious in nature
remains a possibility, and would be consistent with many of the
symptoms. However, the progression of symptoms over time is a much
better fit for a common cold. In particular, the sore throat early on,
then improvement to it, then an increase in mucus secretion 24 hours
later, fit the common cold pattern.

With that said, the very cold interim period may have played a role in
weakening my defenses and increasing the amount of energy my body had
to spend keeping itself warm, so it may have made me more susceptible
to falling sick at an exposure level that normally wouldn't have made
me sick.
