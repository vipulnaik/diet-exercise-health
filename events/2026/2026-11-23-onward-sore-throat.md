# 2026-11-23 onward: sore throat

## Symptoms

### Sore throat / dryness

This was the most central symptom. The evolution of the symptom is
discussed in the chronology of events.

### Head/neck/shoulder soreness

I experienced increased soreness of the head, neck, and shoulder. This
was not enough to detract or debilitate me in day-to-day activities
(such as carrying groceries or doing meal prep) but showed up in
reduced performance on hand gripper exercises.

### Increased system-level fatigue and slowness

My jogging attempt on 2026-11-25, the first after the onset of
symptoms, was shorter than usual (10 minutes compared to the usual of over 30
minutes for indoor jogging).

I also felt a little bit slower and lazier when doing cognitive work,
though the effect was more around activation and context switching
than doing tasks when in flow.

### Increased light and sound sensitivity

My light and sound sensitivity were a little higher than usual. For
instance, I decided to put hands over ears in response to sirens from
ambulances passing by, both outdoors and indoors, on Tuesday
2026-11-24 and Wednesday 2026-11-25. This isn't a bad idea in general,
but it's not something I have felt the need to do in the recent
past. I also felt a little more aversion to having the light on late
at night during cooking and meal prep.

### Slightly increased rate of sneezing and runny nose, though nowhere near the stereotype of a respiratory infection

I had a slightly increased rate of sneezing and runny nose. However,
this was nowhere near the level of a respiratory infection such as the
common cold (with the [June 2025 respiratory
infection](../2025/2025-06-respiratory-infection-likely-common-cold.md)
being my reference for comparison). However, the sneezing and runny
nose did seem, at least in part, related to the sore throat. I discuss
this more in the context of my Trader Joe's trip on 2026-11-24 later
in the document.

### No changes to food taste or smell

With my [June 2025 respiratory
infection](../2025/2025-06-respiratory-infection-likely-common-cold.md),
I had, prior to getting the runny nose, experienced weird changes to
the taste and smell of food. I did not experience any changes to the
taste and smell of food this time around. This was something I was
deliberately on the lookout for.

## Chronology of events

### Monday 2026-11-23: initial symptoms during the day plus a trip in the cold

On Monday 2026-11-23, some time after my first meal, I experienced a
dry, sore throat. It wasn't clear to me whether this was induced by
diet, or random fluctuation, or something to do with the cold weather
over the past few days, or a combination.

I had to go to the Amazon Hub Locker at ASUC Berkeley for package
pickup. This trip was made in the context of an outdoor temperature of
around 53 degrees Fahrenheit. While I didn't find it too
uncomfortable, it likely added to my cold exposure.

### Overnight sleep from 2026-11-23 to 2026-11-24, with drooling and increase in sore throat sensation

Due to a bunch of TODO items, I went to sleep pretty late at night
going from Monday 2026-11-23 to Tuesday 2026-11-24. Fortunately, I
didn't have to get up early the next day, so I slept in and got up
without an alarm, getting about 8 hours 44 minutes from the time of
going to sleep to waking up.

However, it was pretty cold at night (the indoor temperature shortly
before I went to sleep was 60 degrees Fahrenheit, and it was similar
when I woke up, but I suspect the night-time minimum was lower). As
can sometimes happen at these cold temperatures, I drooled quite a
bit, more so when sleeping on my right side. I suspect this drooling
led to mouth drying and exacerbated my dry, sore throat sensation.

### Tuesday 2026-11-24: protective measures

On Tuesday 2026-11-24, now that the sore throat sensation was
relatively strong, I decided to take several protective
measures. While I still considered this likely due to some mix of diet
and cold, there was no single factor I could point to, and an
infection was plausible. Given the recent timeline (no recent close
contact with others, even the most recent store trip before onset of
symptoms was last Thursday 2026-11-19, which is a long gap) I doubted
it was a contagious infection I got from somebody else, but general
precautions still made sense.

#### Trader Joe's trip

I did not have enough visible symptoms to be concerned about having an
infection that was contagious enough to pass on to others, so I made
my Trader Joe's trip as usual. I masked up in the store as usual, so
there was no change to my habits, though I do feel that masking was
more desirable in this context, while normally it's just me exercising
an abundance of caution.

I didn't sneeze or have a runny nose while at Trader Joe's. However, I
did sneeze a couple of times on the long walk back (both were
controlled rather than completely unexpected sneezes). This suggests
that the extent of runny nose was a little higher than usual, but
still at a level within my control.

On a potentially unrelated note, I did have a sense of high smoke
levels on the way to Trader Joe's, and I masked up for a few minutes
to reduce smoke exposure while passing through some streets. The
masking did produce relief, but I have no idea if my sense of smoke
was genuine, or whether it was somehow an artifact of my symptoms.

#### Supplement routine changes

I made the following changes to my supplement routine with the first
meal:

* I added an extra capsule of n-acetylcysteine (NAC), taking a total
  of two 600 mg capsules instead of one. This was to decongest more
  quickly.

* I took two turmeric capsules (I don't normally take turmeric as a
  supplement, relying only on dietary turmeric).

* I took a Vitamin C chewable (I don't normally take Vitamin C, as I
  get enough from diet).

The evidence suggesting that any of these measures would actually help
is weak, but they are low-risk.

I also made a few changes with my second meal:

* I took a capsule of n-acetylcysteine (NAC). I don't normally take
  NAC with my second meal, limiting it to my first meal.

* I took a turmeric capsule (just one, compared to two with my first
  meal).

* I took a Vitamin C chewable (I don't normally take Vitamin C, as I
  get enough from diet).

#### Diet changes

I also started a pause of sauerkraut. That's because something that
acidic might irritate an already dry, sore throat more.

For eggplant meal prep before my second meal, I skipped adding cayenne
pepper (I added normal levels of other condiments such as turmeric,
cumin, and cinnamon).

#### Exercise changes

I had to skip all exercises for scheduling reasons on the day, so I
didn't need to determine whether the sore throat warranted exercise
reduction.

### Wednesday 2026-11-25: continued protective measures and reduced exercise levels

By Wednesday 2026-11-25, I was feeling somewhat better. It also helped
that the weather got warmer, and I was able to sleep in again. I
didn't have any drooling that I noticed.

#### Supplement changes

I followed exactly the same routine of extra supplements as I had
followed the previous day.

#### Diet changes

I continued my sauerkraut pause (and also continued to benefit from
the omission of cayenne pepper in the previous day's eggplant meal
prep).

#### Exercise changes

The day was relaxed enough that I was actually able to fit in exercise
timing-wise.

* I did hand gripper exercises as usual, following the [hand gripper
  exercise
  rotation](../../profile-information/physical-exercise/hand-gripper-exercises-with-history.md). This
  happened to be Day 1 with the outer loop persistence mode being the
  repeated close persistent mode. This means that my goal was to do
  repeated closes of the 150 lb and 100 lb hand grippers as many times
  as I could.

  For the 150 lb hand gripper, I had slightly fewer repetitions than
  usual for both the right hand and the left hand, which I attributed
  to neck/shoulder stress directly linked to the sore throat and
  associated fatigue. The number of repetitions for 100 lbs was normal
  or even a little higher than normal, which suggests that the 100 lb
  hand gripper was not at a threshold where the neck/shoulder fatigue
  was capacity-limiting.

* I did indoor jogging (jogging was forced indoors because I was
  multiplexing it with rice prep), but only for 10 minutes; I felt a
  general system-wide lethargic fatigue at that point (this is
  different from my normal exhaustion fatigue accompanied by leg
  exhaustion with lactate/acid pooling after the full duration of over
  30 minutes). I deliberately chose not to push it as I wanted to give
  priority to enough rest as to recover more quickly.

* I skipped strength exercises in light of the information I got from
  the aborted indoor jogging about my energy level.
