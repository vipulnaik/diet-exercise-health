# 2017-11-18: dentist trip in India

On 2017-11-18, I went to a dentist in Bengaluru to get a dental
cleaning. These are the notes from the dentist.

```
Vipul Naik
grade II plaque accumulation
Advised oral prophylaxis and followup
and bleaching & discolored teeth
Rec
  - Hiora G.A.
    gum astringent
```

I experienced pain and bleeding during the cleaning, and remember
spitting in the bowl a few times. In principle, this was an early
warning sign of the dental health issues that would lead to the
[toothache and head/neck/jaw pain in
2018](../2018/2018-12-head-neck-jaw-tooth-pain.md). However, while the
dentist probably did mention a few things regarding dental hygiene,
the lack of long-term relationship potential (as I was only
temporarily in India and flying back to the United States) probably
led him to put more emphasis on medical solutions like the gum
astringent and tooth whitening. I also may have failed to grasp the
significance of the information presented.

## Gum astringent

The dentist prescribed a gum astringent for gum pain that I got as a
result of the cleaning (I did not have gum pain prior to the
cleaning). I used some of the astringent in the days after the
cleaning and carried the rest back with me to the United States. I
would use it later during the [2018 head/neck/jaw/tooth
pain](../2018/2018-12-head-neck-jaw-tooth-pain.md)

UPDATE 2026-10-28: I dug a little bit more into this. It appears that
while gum astringents are commonly used in India as a way to deal with
gum disease, the Western approach (as I experienced in my care in
recent years) is focused on a mix of regular dental hygiene (flossing
and rinsing) and mechanical plaque removal (through a mix of regular
dental cleaning and deep cleaning / periodontal therapy when
needed). The dentist choosing to prescribe an astringent was therefore
partly just a reflection of standard practice in India.

My own assessment seems to be that astringents are not worth adding in
regular dental care if I am already doing all the expected pieces of
dental hygiene, getting regular cleanings, getting a relatively clean
bill of health during the cleanings, and not experiencing pain or
bleeding. Where they could be helpful is for symptom relief in case of
significant pain or bleeding, but not as a substitute for the actions
that would address the pain or bleeding long-term. The use case covers
both recovering from gingivitis or periodontitis (having already fixed
habits, but still on the recovery route) or acute mechanical
trauma. Use of astringents should ideally be limited to a few days
when the pain and bleeding are most acute.

There's more related discussion of both gum astringents and toothpick
use in India in a [private Claude

## Tooth whitening

I also received a tooth whitening thing (this was through a followup
step so it wasn't included in the prescription; I don't have an
explicit record of what it was) that I tried for a few days. However,
it caused a bit of a sore throat and given my upcoming flight, I
decided to pause it to avoid discomfort while flying. Unlike the gum
astringent, I left it behind in India.
